# Family Finder DNA review

22 November 2026 · [Profile](../README.md#11-profile) · [Testing protocol](../README.md#2-testing) · [Measured health results](../results.md)

The clearest everyday finding is a tendency to digest less lactose. There are also common markers linked to heart disease, hair loss and other traits. For inherited disease, only 32 of 67,367 selected ClinVar variant records met the matching rules below. None showed the listed alternate allele. That limited overlap leaves most inherited risks unanswered.

The polygenic analyses used 445,028 of the 685,439 called autosomal/X markers. Two prostate cancer scores ranked above the reference median. The atrial fibrillation models gave different ranks, and the Alzheimer's scores left a wide range. The hair-loss score was around the 90th percentile among 145 reference men. See the [disease comparisons](#prostate-cancer-atrial-fibrillation-alzheimers-and-coeliac-disease), [other scores](#polygenic-scores) and [separate Y review](#what-the-y-download-adds).

## What matters for this protocol

- **Dairy tolerance.** Two linked markers support a tendency to digest less lactose in adulthood. The lactose-free kefir and skyr in the diet already fit. If ordinary milk causes symptoms, compare the same portion with lactose-free milk. Keep dairy that agrees with you. Persistent symptoms or a raised stool calprotectin deserve their own evaluation. [Lactase study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11788828/) · [NIDDK dietary guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/lactose-intolerance/eating-diet-nutrition)
- **Heart health.** The export contains common markers associated with coronary artery disease and atrial fibrillation. For prevention, use blood pressure, lipids, smoking history and family history to assess overall risk. If palpitations or an irregular-rhythm alert recur, bring the recording to a clinician for ECG review. The DNA findings alone do not justify medication or extra screening. [Coronary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2711874/) · [AF study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4009240/) · [European AF guideline](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae176)
- **Prostate health.** One common marker has a small association with prostate cancer. The more useful detail at a PSA review is the current dutasteride schedule, because dutasteride can lower PSA. Tell the clinician when treatment started and how often it is taken so they can interpret the trend. [Prostate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3526158/) · [Polish Findarts product information](https://aurovitas.pl/PRODUKTY/RX/FINDARTS/Findarts_SmPC_PL_var011_cln.pdf)
- **Family history.** A known disease-causing variant in a relative, several related cancers, or cancer at an unusually young age is a reason to discuss clinical genetics even when the relevant marker is missing here. Bring the relative's report if available. Confirm a medically consequential consumer result in a clinical laboratory before acting on it. [NCI guidance](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet) · [Clinical confirmation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29565420/)

### Aging and telomere length

A large study combined five measures of aging, including parental lifespan, survival to old age and frailty. Seven of its 52 lead markers were called here. At SLC22A3, `rs9364552 CG` carries a G associated with a slightly more favorable combined measure. At `rs980183 AG`, G was associated with a slightly less favorable measure. Each difference was small even at the group level. Two of the seven markers were in the APOE region, including `rs405509 GT`. Neither supplies the missing ε4 result. [Five-trait aging study, Supplementary Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-023-00455-5)

None of the 12 main lead markers in a parental-lifespan study was covered. The one available lead marker in a separate healthspan study, `rs2860197 AG`, failed replication. These studies give no reliable estimate of years of life or years free from disease for this individual. [Parental lifespan, Table 1](https://elifesciences.org/articles/39856#tbl1) · [Healthspan, Table 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-019-0290-0/tables/2)

Sixteen of 197 lead markers were called in a study of white blood cell telomere length. At STN1, `rs10748858 TT` carries the allele associated with shorter average telomeres. At the DKC1 region on X, `rs4898396 CC` represents one copy of C, associated with longer average telomeres. The authors flagged the HBB region as an assay artifact, so its call was excluded from interpretation. The export contains no measurement of telomere length or its change over time. [Telomere study, Supplementary Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8492471/#MOESM4)

## Polygenic scores

A polygenic score weights the alleles at sites specified by a published model. How closely the array reproduces that number and how well the model predicts an outcome are separate questions. The comparisons below used the autosomal/X download. None of these models specifies a Y marker.

### Prostate cancer, atrial fibrillation, Alzheimer’s and coeliac disease

The seven scores below used 433,628 distinct called markers, including nearby markers used to estimate missing sites. Of these, **281,938 were new** beyond the 163,090 used by the earlier four scores. This brings the combined total to **445,028 of 685,439** (65%). Many of those markers are correlated or carry very small weights.

Each percentile compares the estimated score with 313 Finnish, Iberian and Tuscan reference participants, or the 145 men in those groups for prostate cancer. Parentheses show how far the rank could move using the middle 95% of reconstruction errors observed in the same group. These are comparison groups. Personal ancestry, age-specific disease probability and lifetime risk were not estimated.

The AF scores also disagreed in people with complete reference genotypes. Across the 313 participants the two complete scores correlated at 0.64. For 41 people (13%), their percentile ranks differed by at least as much as the 42-point gap seen here. Averaging the two estimates would hide that uncertainty.

**How the scores were checked.** The local reconstruction used the [same training and held-out groups and nearby-marker method](#hair-loss-type-2-diabetes-and-testosterone) as the earlier scores. The reference genotypes covered every weighted site in all seven models. Correlations between estimated and complete scores, in table order, were 0.961 and 0.956 for prostate cancer, 0.974 and 0.980 for AF, 0.822 and 0.907 for the non-APOE Alzheimer’s scores, and 0.942 for coeliac disease. These correlations measure reconstruction accuracy. The AUCs in the table measure disease discrimination in studies with actual diagnoses (0.50 is chance). Neither measure calibrates individual risk.

The two largest models used the original GRCh37 weights, matched to the published rsID, position and allele pair. Both complete reference sums were independently reproduced in 2,490 people. Where a nearby marker could not predict a dosage, the training-group mean filled 29 prostate sites, 31 AF-or-flutter sites and two rare sites in the 83-site Alzheimer’s score. For the 83-site Alzheimer’s score, the high-coverage GRCh38 reference supplied 82 sites, including four insertions/deletions. Ensembl Phase 3 genotypes supplied the final site. All personal calculations ran locally. ### Coronary heart disease

The estimated **GRS27 score was 3.47**. It ranked around the 77th percentile among 503 participants in the European 1000 Genomes panel. This panel is shown because the disease model was validated in European-ancestry cohorts. The person's ancestry was not established here. Errors observed in the European validation subset would put the same estimate anywhere from about the **35th to the 97th percentile**. That is too wide to assign a risk group. Neither 3.47 nor the percentile gives a chance of developing heart disease. Independent Finnish and US cohorts found that a one-standard-deviation increase in the complete score was associated with about a 20% higher relative hazard of coronary disease. For personal prevention decisions, use the measured lipids, blood pressure and clinical history. [Published score](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS000010/) · [Independent evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw450) · [Measured results](../results.md#cardiac-health--coagulation)

Three of the 27 score sites were called directly. To estimate the other 24, the local analysis used 19,472 distinct nearby markers. At each site it multiplied the expected number of effect alleles by the published log-odds weight, then added the 27 contributions. The two LPA sites were inferred. The measured Lp(a) of 7.16nmol/L is the result to use for that biomarker. A much larger coronary score, metaGRS, had only 192,076 of 1,745,179 sites directly usable under the matching rules (11%). It was not scored. [GRS27 weights](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS000010/) · [metaGRS](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS000018/)

**Reconstruction check.** Local Beagle 5.5 imputation used GRCh37, a ±1.5Mb window at each site and non-palindromic, single-base array markers with matching coordinates and compatible alleles. Across 26 populations, 2,002 Phase 3 participants supplied the reference haplotypes. Another 502 were held out, reduced to the same available array sites and scored without their known phase. The estimated and reference-genotype scores correlated at 0.978 overall and 0.962 among the 100 European holdouts (range across five groups 0.917–0.989). The European percentile range above adds the middle 95% of observed reference-minus-estimate differences to the raw score, giving 3.01–3.92. It describes a technical check on public genotypes. It does not measure clinical prediction accuracy or test the laboratory accuracy of the original array. [Beagle 5.5](https://faculty.washington.edu/browning/beagle/beagle.html) · [Phase 3 reference](https://www.internationalgenome.org/category/phase-3/)

The local run used the 27 harmonized GRCh37 positions, the Beagle 27Feb25.75f release, the GRCh37 genetic map, two threads per locus and seed 274381. A seeded draw held out 20% of each population (rounded to the nearest person) from sorted sample IDs in population-name order. Strand checks used the original association study, including forward A at rs3825807 and forward C at rs216172. The latter's discovery frequency was 0.37, while the score table lists 0.64. A second calculation from the source odds ratios, original reference genotypes and inferred allele counts reproduced the score and the validation results. [Harmonized score file](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/pgs/scores/PGS000010/ScoringFiles/Harmonized/PGS000010_hmPOS_GRCh37.txt.gz) · [Discovery study, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3119261/#T2)

| Published score | Score sites measured / total | Personal result and interpretation |

| [Type 2 diabetes](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS001818/) | 15,394/30,745 | The raw score was **0.417**, around the **65th percentile** among 313 reference participants (roughly 49th–83rd after reconstruction error). | [Total testosterone in men](https://www.pgscatalog.org/score/PGS001988/) | 1,978/3,985 | The raw score was **−0.00253**, around the **5th percentile** among the same 145 men (below the lowest reference value to about the 11th percentile after reconstruction error). The public reference contained every weighted site in all three models. The reconstruction learned how to estimate missing sites from nearby, directly called markers in 190 UK and Utah reference participants (GBR and CEU). The 313 people from Finland, Spain and Italy were held out. Using the person's available sites, reconstructed and complete hair scores correlated at 0.975 among the 145 held-out men, compared with 0.732 using only directly measured score sites. The hair calculation used **69,287 distinct array markers** including nearby sites. The equivalent correlations for testosterone were 0.975 and 0.814 in the men, and for diabetes 0.965 and 0.732 in all 313 people. These checks establish how well the scores were reconstructed. In the original UK and Irish evaluation, the complete hair score's adjusted correlation with reported balding was 0.466. [Reference panel](https://privefl.github.io/bigsnpr/reference/download_1000G.html) · [Prediction study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764121/)

For each unmeasured site, the local analysis selected up to eight directly called markers within 250kb on either side, ranked by absolute covariance with that site in the training participants after standardizing each predictor. It fitted a ridge regression (penalty 0.15), bounded the estimated effect-allele count to 0–2 and applied the published weight. Two testosterone sites used the training mean. Direct matches required the GRCh37 position, rsID and compatible alleles. Strand-ambiguous array sites were excluded as inputs. Percentile ranges use the middle 95% of observed differences between reconstructed and complete scores. They apply to the stated reference groups. For example, the same diabetes score ranked around the 52nd percentile across all 2,300 held-out participants combined. With the same method, hair-score reconstruction correlations ranged from 0.847 to 0.969 across four other ancestry groups.

### What the Y download adds

The 13,038 called Y rows covered 12,872 distinct positions. Some positions had more than one marker name. To check the coordinate system, ten evenly spaced rsIDs were compared with Ensembl. All nine with a Y mapping matched GRCh38, and the tenth had no primary Y mapping. The separate Y comparison therefore used the GRCh38 ClinVar release. [Example GRCh38 mapping](https://rest.ensembl.org/variation/human/rs11575897?content-type=application/json) · [GRCh37 comparison](https://grch37.rest.ensembl.org/variation/human/rs11575897?content-type=application/json)

Under the germline classification rules below, ClinVar listed one eligible Y single-base variant with at least two review stars and 19 in the single-submitter group. None of those 20 positions appeared in the Y file. A missing position supplies no information about whether the variant is present. [Dated GRCh38 ClinVar release](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pub/clinvar/vcf_GRCh38/weekly/clinvar_20261115.vcf.gz)

The file is useful for paternal ancestry. Its called markers and gaps cannot establish a fertility-related AZF deletion. Studies of age-related loss of Y in blood used quantitative signals that are absent from this export. The Y results therefore give no personal estimate of fertility or longevity. [FamilyTreeDNA haplogroups](https://help.familytreedna.com/hc/en-us/articles/14825216536207-Family-Finder-Haplogroups) · [European Y microdeletion testing guidance](https://doi.org/10.1111/andr.13514) · [UK Biobank study of Y loss](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6098142/)

### ClinVar comparison for chromosomes 1–22 and X

The offline comparison read all 4,620,718 records in the 15 November 2026 GRCh37 ClinVar release. It selected single-base alternate alleles on chromosomes 1–22 and X with at least two review stars and a germline classification of pathogenic, likely pathogenic (including low penetrance), or established or likely risk allele. The stars describe agreement or expert review of the database entry. They do not measure the accuracy of this DNA test. [ClinVar classifications](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/docs/clinsig/) · [ClinVar review levels](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/docs/review_status/)

Of the 67,367 eligible allele records, 67,224 had no matching position in the export. Among the other exclusions, 96 had a provider-specific marker name, nine had strand-ambiguous alleles (A/T or C/G), four lacked an exact rsID match, one had incompatible letters and one was a no-call. The remaining 32 had the expected rsID and position and showed only the reference allele. Several variant records can refer to the same gene or condition. The common trait associations above were reviewed separately using human studies.

A separate check used the same rules for lower-confidence records with criteria from a single submitter. Of 113,242 eligible records, 14 could be compared and all showed only the reference allele. Neither comparison can rule out a disease or establish carrier status.

To repeat the comparison, pass the local autosomal/X download and the dated ClinVar file to the [offline review tool](../tools/review_dna_overlap.py). Add `--single-submitter` for the second group. The tool prints the counts and any alternate-allele matches for review. The NCBI archive used here had MD5 `ab8eca05a7af6f5f3512c98edc869e79`. [Dated ClinVar download](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pub/clinvar/vcf_GRCh37/weekly/clinvar_20261115.vcf.gz)

```powershell
# From the repository root, with the dated ClinVar file in Downloads
$exportPath = (Get-Item DNA/37_*.csv.gz).FullName
$referencePath = Join-Path $env:USERPROFILE 'Downloads/clinvar_20261115.vcf.gz'
python -B tools/review_dna_overlap.py $exportPath $referencePath
```
